The problem

Four failures that repeat, in every sector, for the same reason.

Each of these is a capability gap, not a software gap. An ERP project that starts without closing them ends with the old spreadsheet still running.

The board cannot interrogate technology

Technology reaches the board as a status update rather than as a business case, so the hard question is not asked until the money is spent.

The executive cannot specify

Requirements arrive as a wish list rather than a defined outcome, so the vendor scopes the work and the organisation inherits whatever the vendor thought best.

The middle cannot absorb

A system goes live and the parallel spreadsheet survives, because the process was never redesigned around the system.

The technical team cannot advance

Capable engineers spend their time keeping yesterday running, so under‑budgeting and mounting technical risk surface only when something fails.

The model

Three domains, one governed data foundation.

The three domains are transformed together, on one governed data foundation. Change one alone and the other two pull it back.

What changes

Processes, models, relationships

Business processes – how the work is actually done.
Business models – how the organisation earns and competes.
Customer relationships – internal employees or external customers.

One unified system

Data – governed, single source

People. Process. Platform. Policy. The four held together by one data foundation everybody trusts – which, in practice, is what an ERP is for.

What the business gets

Governance, visibility, control

Governance – decisions made on evidence, not opinion.
Visibility – the same picture for everyone who looks.
Control – exceptions found early, not at year end.
